DIY Content Guide

⌂ | DIY Content Guide

Film your own content

You do not need a production budget to start showing up. The phone in your pocket is enough to build trust with your customers. This free guide covers how to set it up, what to film and a simple 30-day plan to follow. When you are ready for more, we are here.

Set up your phone

Five minutes before you hit record makes the biggest difference.

Clean the lens

Wipe it with a soft cloth before every shoot. A smudged lens is the most common reason phone video looks soft or hazy.

Film vertical

Hold the phone upright (9:16) for Reels, TikTok and Shorts. Turn it sideways only for YouTube or your website.

Use the main lens

Stay on the 1x camera. It has the best sensor on the phone. Walk closer instead of zooming in.

Lock focus and exposure

Tap and hold on your subject until the lock appears, so the picture does not shift or flicker while you film.

Turn on the grid

Use it to keep lines straight and to place your subject a third of the way into the frame, not dead center.

Silence the phone

Turn on Do Not Disturb so a call or notification does not interrupt a good take.

Light, sound, steady

Light

  • Face a window. Soft daylight on the face is the most flattering light there is.
  • Never film with a bright window behind someone. They will turn into a silhouette.
  • Avoid harsh overhead lights. Outdoors, early morning and late afternoon look best.

Sound

  • People will forgive average video. They will not sit through bad audio.
  • Film in a quiet room with soft surfaces (rugs, curtains, furniture) to cut echo.
  • An inexpensive clip-on microphone that plugs into or pairs with your phone is the best upgrade you can make.

Steady shots

  • Rest the phone on something or use a small tripod. Shaky footage reads as amateur.
  • Keep the camera at eye level when someone is talking.
  • Hold every shot for at least five seconds and move slowly. You can always cut it shorter.

Extra footage

  • Film the details: hands at work, tools, products, the sign out front, the street.
  • These short clips (called b-roll) cover cuts and keep viewers watching.
  • Grab ten of them every time you film. They are the easiest thing to reuse.

How to structure a clip

Most good short videos follow the same four steps. Plan them before you film.

  1. HookThe first two seconds. Start with the result, a bold line or a question. No logos, no "hey guys".
  2. ShowLet people see it: the work, the product, the process, the people.
  3. Say whyOne or two sentences on why it matters to the customer.
  4. Next stepTell them what to do: call, visit, book, or follow for more.

What to film

Never run out of ideas. Every one of these works for almost any business.

  • Meet the owner: who you are and why you started
  • A tour of your space
  • How it is made, start to finish
  • Before and after
  • Answer a question customers always ask
  • A day in the life
  • A quick tip from your expertise
  • Meet the team
  • A customer shares their experience (with permission)
  • Your favorite spot in town

Your 30-day plan

Three posts a week for four weeks. Each week has one theme, so you always know what to film next.

Week 1

Introduce yourself

  1. Who you are and why you started
  2. A tour of your space
  3. What makes you different

Week 2

Show the work

  1. A job or product from start to finish
  2. A before and after
  3. A day in the life

Week 3

Answer questions

  1. The question you hear most
  2. A common mistake people make
  3. What a first visit or first job looks like

Week 4

Your people and your town

  1. Meet the team
  2. A customer story (with permission)
  3. A neighbor, a local spot or a community event

The weekly routine

Shoot day

Set aside one hour a week. Film all three posts for the week at once, plus extra b-roll.

Edit day

Spend another hour trimming, adding captions and writing a short description for each post.

Post days

Schedule the three posts across the week. Reply to every comment in the first hour.

Editing

  • Use a free app: CapCut, InShot, or the editor built into your phone.
  • Cut the dead air at the start and end. Get to the point fast.
  • Add captions. Many people scroll with the sound off.
  • Keep most clips between 15 and 60 seconds.
  • Pick a clear cover frame and a short title so your profile looks organized.

Before you hit record

  • Lens cleaned
  • Phone vertical, main lens, grid on
  • Do Not Disturb on, storage space free
  • Facing the light, nothing bright behind you
  • Quiet room, microphone on
  • Phone steady at eye level
  • Hook planned for the first two seconds
  • Ten pieces of b-roll filmed
